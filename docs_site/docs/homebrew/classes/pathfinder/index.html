Pathfinder

“Roads are for people who already know where they’re going.”

The Pathfinder is the one who goes first. Where the maps end, where the trail is cold, where the pass is choked with something that eats scouting parties — that is where a Pathfinder is sent, and where a Pathfinder comes back from. They are the marriage of the soldier and the tracker: the discipline, footwork, and weapon craft of a veteran warrior welded to the instincts of a hunter who has never lost a foe. Armies follow the lines they draw. Caravans survive the passes they clear. Things that hide in the deep places have learned to move when a Pathfinder takes an interest.

Prerequisites​

Level 20 Cycle: Ranger 7+, Fighter 7+

The cycle must consist of exactly 20 levels split between Ranger and Fighter only, with at least 7 levels in each (e.g. 10/10, 13/7, 7/13). No other class levels may appear in the cycle.

Ranger Version: This prestige class is built on X’s Revised (Again) Ranger. Its features assume the Favored Foe and Warden Specialization systems from that revision.

Hit Die: 1d10 per Pathfinder level

PL 21: Pathfinder Skill​

You gain proficiency in Wisdom (Survival) and Wisdom (Perception). If you are already proficient in one or both, you gain Expertise in it instead, doubling your proficiency bonus for ability checks using it.

You can also choose one other skill from the Ranger or Fighter skill list, gaining proficiency in it.

PL 21: Prestige Scaling​

This class is measured in Pathfinder Levels (PfL), numbered 1 through 20 as you advance in it. Your Prestige Level (PL) is always 20 + your Pathfinder Level — it exists to describe your total standing among prestige classes and is what features in this class mean when they scale off “your Prestige Level” (Prestige Level 21 through 40, matching PfL 1 through 20).

Your Pathfinder Save DC is 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your Wisdom modifier.

PL 21: Ascendant Spellcasting​

Your Ranger spellcasting continues past where your qualifying cycle left off, keyed to your Ranger levels rather than your total character level.

Calculate your Effective Caster Level: your Ranger levels + your Pathfinder Level (PfL), to a maximum of 33. A 7th-level Ranger entering this class at PfL 1 has an Effective Caster Level of 8; a 13th-level Ranger entering at PfL 1 has an Effective Caster Level of 14. Either way, your first Pathfinder level always picks up exactly one step past where your Ranger spellcasting left off, and every Pathfinder level thereafter advances it by one more — a 13th-level Ranger reaches the table’s cap of 33 at PfL 20, no matter how it’s split.

Consult the Pathfinder Ascendant Casting table below using your Effective Caster Level. This replaces your Ranger slots and Spells Known entirely — don’t stack it with your base Ranger table.

Wisdom remains your spellcasting ability. As normal, whenever you gain a Pathfinder level you can swap one Ranger spell you know for another.

Rows 8–20 are the standard Ranger half-caster progression, unchanged. PL 21: Perfected Favored Foe​

When your Favored Foe dies, you can use a Reaction to designate a new creature within 60 feet as your Favored Foe. You can also designate a creature whose tracks or remains you have examined within the last hour, even if you cannot see it.

Your attack bonus against your Favored Foe is equal to your Wisdom modifier (minimum +2), if it is not already.

Once per turn when you hit your Favored Foe with a weapon attack, it takes extra damage equal to one roll of your Trailblazer Die.

While your Favored Foe is on the same plane of existence, you know its direction and rough distance at all times, and you have advantage on checks to track or find it.

Your attacks against your Favored Foe ignore all cover except Total Cover, and are unaffected by the Invisible condition or by magical concealment.

PL 22: Unerring Step​

You gain the Land’s Stride feature if you do not have it. If you do, it improves: difficult terrain of any kind — natural, magical, or conjured — never costs you extra movement, and you leave no tracks unless you choose to. You gain a Climb Speed and Swim Speed equal to your Speed, and you can move across liquid, snow, and other unstable surfaces without sinking, provided you end your turn on solid ground.

Additionally, you can use the Search action as a Bonus Action.

PL 22: Second Wind (Prestige)​

Your Second Wind now restores Hit Points equal to 2d10 + your Prestige Level. When you use Second Wind, you may also end one of the following conditions on yourself: Frightened, Poisoned, or Prone.

PL 23: Warden’s Continuum​

You gain the next tier of your Warden Specialization that your qualifying cycle hadn’t reached — and only that one. If your Ranger levels were below 9th, you gain Warden’s Covenant (9th). If your Ranger levels were 9th or higher, you gain Warden’s Pinnacle (14th) instead.

You cannot switch to a different Warden Specialization with this feature

PL 23: Adopted Discipline​

Whatever subclass you finished your qualifying cycle with stops there — it does not keep progressing on its own. The only way to unlock any further subclass tier is through this feature.

Choose any Ranger archetype or any Fighter subclass — the one you already had, or a new one entirely. You gain its features at the matching Pathfinder Level:

Ranger archetype: Pathfinder Level 3rd, 7th, 11th, and 15th.

Fighter subclass: Pathfinder Level 3rd, 7th, 10th, 15th, and 18th.

PL 24: Ability Score Improvement​

At Pathfinder Level 4, 8, 12, 16, and 19, you can increase one ability score by 2, or two ability scores by 1, to a maximum of 20 (unless another feature allows otherwise). You may take a Feat instead.

PL 25: Hounding Strikes​

When you take the Attack action, you can make one additional attack with the weapon you are wielding, provided at least one of the attacks in that action targets your Favored Foe.

This is not an Extra Attack feature and does not interact with the rule against stacking them. It adds one attack to however many the Attack action already grants you, whether that is two or three.

PL 25: Ascendant Surge​

You gain one additional use of Action Surge between rests. When you use Action Surge, you can also move up to your Speed without provoking Opportunity Attacks, and the first attack you make during the surged action against your Favored Foe is a Critical Hit on a roll of 18–20.

PL 26: Relentless Pursuit​

When a creature you can see within 60 feet moves, you can take a Reaction to move up to half your Speed toward it. If you end this movement within 30 feet of your Favored Foe, you may make one weapon attack against it as part of that Reaction.

Additionally, you can follow your Favored Foe through teleportation or onto the Ethereal Plane — no other form of planar travel. When it teleports or shifts to the Ethereal Plane while you are within 60 feet, you may immediately follow, appearing in an unoccupied space within 10 feet of it. You can only return to the space you left (or the nearest unoccupied space to it) once your Favored Foe is dead, as a Bonus Action.

PL 27: Trailcraft Evasion​

You gain the Evasion feature: when you make a Dexterity saving throw against an effect that deals half damage on a success, you instead take no damage on a success and half damage on a failure.

Additionally, Opportunity Attacks against you are made with disadvantage.

PL 29: The Foe’s Undoing​

Your Favored Foe’s defenses fail against you. Your attacks against it ignore Resistance to the damage type dealt, and reduce Immunity to Resistance. Your Favored Foe has disadvantage on saving throws against your Ranger spells and Pathfinder features.

Additionally, your Primeval Awareness has no daily limit, and its range extends to 20 miles.

PL 30: Dominion of the Wild​

You have walked enough of the world that it answers to you. You are immune to Exhaustion from environmental sources and to extreme heat and cold, and you no longer need to eat or drink (you still require a Long Rest’s worth of rest).

You may cast Commune with Nature, Find the Path, and Nondetection (self only) at will, without expending a spell slot or material components.

PL 30: Indomitable (Prestige)​

You gain the Indomitable feature with one use if you do not have it; if you do, you gain one additional use. When you use it, you may instead choose to automatically succeed on the saving throw if the effect came from your Favored Foe. Once you use that improvement, you can’t do so again until you finish a Long Rest.

PL 31: Ceaseless Assault​

Your Extra Attack feature lets you attack three times instead of twice when you take the Attack action. As normal, this does not stack with other features that set your number of Extra Attacks — if you already attack three times (from Fighter’s Extra Attack (2), for example), you instead add your Wisdom modifier to the damage of the final attack in the action.

Hounding Strikes is separate and still adds its attack on top, putting every Pathfinder at four attacks by this level.

PL 33: Ambusher’s Certainty​

When you attack a creature that has not yet taken a turn in combat, or a creature that cannot see you, your attack roll cannot miss on anything other than a natural 1, and you deal an additional Trailblazer Die of damage on a hit.

PL 35: Honed Training​

Your years of experience sharpen into real mastery. Choose two skills you’re proficient in — you gain Expertise in both, doubling your proficiency bonus for ability checks using them.

You also gain a third Fighting Style option (see Fighting Style) and one additional Weapon Mastery property choice.

PL 38: The Untrodden Way​

You can spend 1 minute to open a path where none exists. You and up to twelve willing creatures travel to any location you have personally visited, or any location described to you in precise detail, arriving there after 1d4 hours. The journey passes through a shifting corridor of trails between places; you cannot be intercepted, tracked, or scried during the passage.

Once you use this feature, you cannot use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

PL 38: Peerless Hunter​

You have advantage on all attack rolls against your Favored Foe, and it has disadvantage on all attack rolls against you.

PL 40: First and Last​

You are the first through the door and the last one standing in it.

Foe Slayer. Add your Wisdom modifier to all damage rolls you make against your Favored Foe, weapon and spell alike.

Nowhere Is Far Enough. Your Relentless Pursuit now lets you follow your Favored Foe through any form of planar travel, not just teleportation or the Ethereal Plane.

The Next One. When your Favored Foe drops to 0 Hit Points, you can immediately designate a new creature and make one weapon attack against it, no action required. Once per turn.

Last Standing. When you would be reduced to 0 Hit Points while your Favored Foe remains alive, you instead regain Hit Points equal to your Prestige Level and can immediately move up to your Speed towards your Favored Foe and make one attack against them. Once per Long Rest.
