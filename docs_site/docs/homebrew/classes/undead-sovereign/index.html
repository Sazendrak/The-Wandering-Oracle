Undead Sovereign

An Undead Sovereign commands death beyond the boundaries of ordinary necromancy. Beasts, giants, dragons, and extraplanar creatures become vessels for their power. Their servants sustain them, their command strengthens the dead, and their own body grows increasingly resistant to death.

Prerequisites​

You must complete a pure first cycle—20 levels in a single class—with one of the following subclasses:

  • Wizard: School of Necromancy.
  • Druid: Circle of Spores.
  • Cleric: Death Domain.
  • Paladin: Oathbreaker.
  • Warlock: The Undead, The Undying, or The Hexblade.

Other subclasses require staff approval to qualify.

Class Basics​

Hit Dice: 1d8 per Undead Sovereign level.

Hit Points: 1d8 (or 5) + your Constitution modifier per Undead Sovereign level.

Additional Proficiencies: Two skills of your choice from Arcana, History, Insight, Medicine, and Religion.

Choose Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma as your Sovereign ability when you enter this class. It is used by your Undead Sovereign spells and features.

Sovereign save DC = 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Sovereign ability modifier.

Spell attack modifier = your proficiency bonus + your Sovereign ability modifier.

Subclass Selection and Progression​

When you enter Undead Sovereign, you may choose an additional subclass from the class in which you completed your first cycle. You retain your existing subclass.

You gain the additional subclass’s features when your Undead Sovereign class level reaches the level at which the parent class normally grants them.

For example, an additional wizard subclass grants its features at Undead Sovereign class levels 2, 6, 10, and 14—progression levels 22, 26, 30, and 34.

When an additional subclass feature refers to your level in its parent class, use your Undead Sovereign class level. Your original subclass continues to use your actual levels in its parent class.

This progression grants only the additional subclass’s features. It does not grant other parent-class features, spell slots, or increases to parent-class resources unless the additional subclass feature explicitly grants them.

Progression​

Your progression level equals 20 + your Undead Sovereign class levels. All feature levels below refer to progression level.

Your additional subclass follows the Subclass Selection and Progression rules above.

PL 21: Spellcasting​

You gain spell slots and can prepare spells from the Undead Sovereign spell list.

Spell Slots​

The table shows the number and level of spell slots this feature grants. All these slots are the same level. You regain expended slots when you finish a long rest.

Progression LevelSpell SlotsSlot Level
21–2221st
23–2422nd
25–2623rd
27–2824th
29–3025th
31–3635th
37–4045th

These slots are additional to those granted by your other classes. Undead Sovereign levels do not otherwise advance another class’s spellcasting or spell-slot progression.

Preparing Spells​

When you finish a long rest, prepare a number of spells from the Undead Sovereign spell list equal to your Sovereign ability modifier plus half your Undead Sovereign class level, rounded down (minimum of one spell).

Each spell must be of a level no higher than the slot level shown in the table. Casting a spell does not remove it from your prepared spells.

Spellcasting Ability and Focus​

Your Sovereign ability is your spellcasting ability for these spells. They use your Sovereign save DC and spell attack modifier.

You can use an arcane focus or holy symbol as a spellcasting focus for your Undead Sovereign spells.

Using Your Slots​

You can use these slots to cast your prepared Undead Sovereign spells or spells you know or have prepared through another class, following that spell’s normal casting rules.

You can also expend these slots for Undead Sovereign features, including Draconic Breath recovery and additional uses of Grave Renewal. Slots from your other classes can also pay these costs.

PL 21: Greater Reanimation​

You perform a 10-minute ritual over eligible corpses within 30 feet of you. Each rises as an undead under your control, using its original stat block with the changes below.

A corpse must retain enough of its body to support its attacks and movement. Creatures with class levels and remains produced by temporary summoning effects are ineligible. The ritual does not create equipment.

Reanimation Limits​

New creature types are added to those already unlocked. Reanimation Improvements increase your limits as shown below.

Progression LevelNew Creature TypesMaximum CR per CreatureTotal CR Budget
21Humanoids, Beasts24
25Monstrosities48
29Giants612
33Dragons1416
37Fiends, Celestials1820

You can control up to twice your proficiency bonus in undead through Greater Reanimation. Each must meet your maximum CR per creature, and their combined CR cannot exceed your total CR budget.

Use each creature’s original CR before reanimation, counting CR below 1 as CR 1.

The total CR of creatures you animate between long rests also cannot exceed your budget. Destroying or releasing undead does not refund this allowance. Renewing control does not spend it.

These limits apply only to Greater Reanimation.

Reanimated Creatures​

Use the creature’s original stat block with these changes:

  • Its creature type becomes Undead.
  • Its hit-point maximum becomes half its original average hit points, rounded down (minimum 1). Apply bonuses afterward. It rises with this many hit points.
  • Its Intelligence becomes 6 if it was higher. It understands your languages and can speak any languages it could speak in life.
  • It gains immunity to poison damage and the poisoned condition. It requires no air, food, drink, or sleep.
  • It retains its size, Armor Class, movement, senses, other ability scores, saving throw and skill bonuses, damage resistances, damage immunities, damage vulnerabilities, and condition immunities.
  • It retains weapon attacks and Multiattack. Retained attacks keep their damage, reach, range, and any effects that grapple, restrain through a grapple, knock prone, or swallow. They lose all other additional effects. Multiattack allows only retained weapon attacks.
  • It loses all original traits and all other original actions, bonus actions, and reactions, including spellcasting, Legendary Resistance, legendary actions, mythic actions, and lair actions.
  • If it is proficient with a weapon it gains the ability to use that weapons mastery.
  • It can take ordinary actions such as Dash, Disengage, and Dodge, make opportunity attacks, and use abilities granted by Undead Sovereign.

Prepare its modified stat block when you animate it and reuse that stat block during play.

Initiative​

Undead created through Greater Reanimation take their turns immediately after yours. You choose their order each round. Each has its own movement, action, bonus action, and reaction.

Control​

Your control lasts for 24 hours. Another 10-minute ritual renews control over any number of these undead within 30 feet for a further 24 hours.

You can release an undead at any time without an action. If released or your control expires, it becomes an inanimate corpse.

This feature cannot take control of another creature’s undead.

PL 21: Grave Command​

As a bonus action, mentally issue orders to any or all undead under your control within 60 feet. They follow those orders on their turns until completed or replaced. Without orders, undead created through Greater Reanimation defend themselves.

Orders can direct any action available to the undead, including Draconic Breath. The undead must expend any required use.

This replaces their usual command range and action requirement. It does not extend their existence or your control.

At progression level 27, the range increases to 120 feet. When you use this bonus action, you can also choose one commanded undead. It can immediately use its reaction to move up to half its speed without provoking opportunity attacks.

This movement still requires your bonus action when you gain Deathless Command.

PL 22: Greater Grim Harvest​

When you or an undead under your control reduces a creature within 60 feet of you to 0 hit points, you can regain hit points equal to your Sovereign ability modifier plus twice your proficiency bonus.

Once you use this feature, you cannot use it again until the start of your next turn.

Constructs, Undead, and creatures you summoned or control do not trigger this healing. You cannot benefit from both this feature and Grim Harvest on the same turn.

At progression level 35, the healing increases to your Sovereign ability modifier plus three times your proficiency bonus. Any healing beyond your hit-point maximum becomes temporary hit points, which last for 1 minute.

PL 23: Graveborn Resilience​

You gain resistance to necrotic and poison damage, and advantage on saving throws to avoid or end the poisoned condition.

At progression level 31, you become immune to poison damage and the poisoned condition.

PL 24: Ability Score Improvement​

At progression levels 24, 28, 32, 36, and 39, increase one ability score by 2, or two ability scores by 1 each.

You cannot increase a score above 20 using this feature. Any option to choose a feat instead follows TWR’s rules.

PL 25: Unhallowed Might​

Weapon attacks made by undead under your control count as magical for overcoming resistance and immunity to nonmagical attacks and damage.

When one of these undead hits with a weapon attack, you can replace all of that attack’s damage types with necrotic damage. Choose before rolling damage; the amount is unchanged.

PL 26: Eternal Legion​

During a 10-minute ritual, designate up to your proficiency bonus in undead you created and currently control as your Eternal Legion.

Your control over them no longer expires with time. This does not extend a temporary summon’s duration or prevent another effect from ending your control. Undead created through Greater Reanimation still count toward its limits.

You can replace members during another 10-minute ritual. Removing a member ends this indefinite control; any unexpired control from its original spell or feature remains.

You cannot have more members of your Eternal Legion than your proficiency bonus.

PL 29: Withering Power​

When you or an undead under your control deals necrotic damage to a creature, you can prevent that creature from regaining hit points until the start of your next turn.

Once you use this effect, you cannot use it again until the start of your next turn.

You can also cast animate dead at 3rd level and create undead at 6th level once each without expending a spell slot or preparing the spell. You regain both castings when you finish a long rest. Their normal components, casting times, and restrictions apply.

These castings use your Sovereign ability. This feature does not add create undead to your prepared spells or grant a 6th-level spell slot.

Undead created by these spells follow their normal rules, except where another Undead Sovereign feature explicitly changes them.

PL 30: Beyond Undeath​

You become immune to necrotic damage. Your hit-point maximum cannot be reduced, and you cannot be aged magically.

When you would drop to 0 hit points without being killed outright, you can destroy an undead under your control within 120 feet that has at least 1 hit point. You drop to 1 hit point instead. This requires no action.

Once you preserve yourself this way, you cannot do so again until you finish a long rest.

PL 33: Titanic Reanimation​

Undead created through Greater Reanimation gain a bonus to their hit-point maximum equal to twice your Undead Sovereign class level.

Apply this bonus after halving their original hit points. The bonus updates as you gain levels in this class.

Draconic Breath​

When you animate a dragon through Greater Reanimation, choose one damaging breath weapon it possessed in life that used a cone or line. If it had an eligible breath weapon, it gains the following action:

Draconic Breath. As an action, the dragon exhales in a 30-foot cone or a 60-foot-long, 5-foot-wide line, matching the chosen breath’s shape.

Each creature in the area must make a saving throw against your Sovereign save DC, using the original breath’s saving throw ability. A creature takes 8d8 damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success.

The breath retains its original damage type, but loses its additional effects and recharge.

Shared Breath Uses​

You have three uses of Draconic Breath shared among all dragons under your control that possess this action. Each activation expends one use.

You regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest. As a bonus action, you can expend a spell slot of 3rd level or higher to restore one expended use, up to a maximum of three available uses.

Creating, replacing, or reanimating undead does not restore uses.

PL 34: Sovereign Command​

Starting at 34th level, you can use magic to bring undead under your control, even those created by other necromancers. As an action, you can choose two undead that you can see within 60 feet of you. That creature must make a Charisma saving throw against your undead sovereign spell save DC. If it succeeds, you can't use this feature on it again. If it fails, it becomes friendly to you and obeys your commands until you use this feature again.

Intelligent undead are harder to control in this way. If the target has an Intelligence of 8 or higher, it has advantage on the saving throw. If it fails the saving throw and has an Intelligence of 12 or higher, it can repeat the saving throw at the end of every hour until it succeeds and breaks free.

PL 37: Grave Renewal​

When you use Greater Grim Harvest, you can give its healing to one undead under your control within 60 feet instead of yourself. Any healing beyond that undead’s hit-point maximum becomes temporary hit points, which last for 1 minute.

You can do so even if you are at your hit-point maximum.

Additionally, as an action, choose a number of undead under your control within 60 feet up to your Sovereign ability modifier (minimum of one). Each regains hit points equal to twice your Undead Sovereign class level.

You can use this action once without expending a spell slot, regaining that use when you finish a long rest. Additional uses each require a spell slot of 5th level or higher, expended as part of the action.

Neither benefit restores a destroyed undead.

PL 38: Deathless Command​

You can issue orders through Grave Command anywhere on the same plane of existence without using an action or bonus action.

The reaction movement granted by Grave Command at progression level 27 gains this range but still requires your bonus action and the undead’s reaction.

Sovereign Command also gains this range but still requires your action action.This feature does not extend an undead’s existence or the duration of your control.

PL 40: Necromantic Ascension​

Your Sovereign ability score and Constitution score each increase by 4, to a maximum of 30.
