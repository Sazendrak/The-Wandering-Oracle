Warmage

THE WARMAGE​

A Complete Class for Fifth Edition

An elf recites the motions for the simplest spell she knows, repeating them until she knows they are perfect. With a single precise stroke she unleashes a wave of arcane energy, focused into a searing blue beam.

A tiefling waves her hands to conjure floating greatswords of humming blue energy that dance about in mesmerizing and deadly arcs. One blade takes on an aspect of fire, the other lightning, as she begins to cleave through the goblinoid horde.

Waving a black-and-red checkered banner, a dragonborn king charges into the fore. At his sides are warmages of all houses, a chessboard of elite arcanists poised to turn the tide of battle.

Cantrip Masters​

While other spellcasters aspire to grander and more complex spells, warmages refine and master the most fundamental magic: cantrips. A warmage wields magic the way a warrior uses a sword, a bow or an axe — as a weapon to be perfected, not as an unknowable force to be feared. In contrast to sorcerers and wizards, their magic is a trainable skill, one that can be mastered by almost anyone with discipline and aptitude. Warmages therefore stand among the most fearsome of spellcasters while commanding only the most rudimentary of spells.

A warmage does not out-damage a wizard. She out-manoeuvres one. The same fire bolt that a first-year apprentice throws becomes, in a warmage’s hands, a cone of flame, a spray of embers that catches three goblins at once, a shot that punches through a stone pillar, or a blast that knocks a troll flat on its back — because she has spent years learning what the spell can be made to do rather than searching for a bigger one.

Strategic Geniuses​

Warmages have a long and storied history as masterful strategists and tacticians, both in grand battles and in personal skirmishes. If a warmage is outgunned or outnumbered, they will try to outthink their foes, which works more often than not. Warmages demonstrate an efficient kind of pragmatism, owing perhaps to their ability to solve many problems with just a few magical tools.

Aligned Free Agents​

While some warmages are fully self-taught, the majority join a house: a loose confederation of warmages who practise together and share secrets of the trade. Houses teach invaluable battlefield strategy, but each house also touts its own brands of tactics and styles of magical trickery. The House of Knights emphasises armour and martial prowess; the House of Kings emphasises battlefield command.

By way of metaphor, houses that value honour and teamwork above all else brand themselves after games of strategy — the Bishops, Kings, Knights, Pawns and Rooks — whereas those who epitomise self-reliance name themselves for games of chance. This division dates back to the earliest warmage masters. Legend has it that the first two masters separated when one decided to found a college; the other, out of spite, built a casino outside its grounds. The gamblers of that casino are the House of Fortune, and they have never once apologised.

Creating a Warmage​

Warmages come from all walks of life, so as you build yours, consider where you first learned the fundamentals of magic. You might have picked up cantrips from a fellow adventurer or in the early stages of wizarding training. You could be entirely self-taught. Choose an Arcane Initiation that best fits your character’s history with magic.

Consider also how you feel about magic as a whole. Are you envious of other spellcasters, or confident you could best them regardless? Did you once pine to be a wizard, but find you could never muster enough true magic to form a spell slot? Did you choose warmagistry to hone your talents to their absolute peak, or to wield power no warrior can equal?

Lastly, consider your prospective House. Though you do not choose one until 3rd level, your aspirations might shape which you favour. If you would like to dabble in greater arcane magic, the House of Bishops is the best fit. If the clashing of swords at the frontline piques your interest, consider the House of Knights. If you would rather not be told which of the two you are, the House of Pawns will have you.

Quick Build​

Make Intelligence your highest ability score, followed by Constitution. Make Strength your third highest if you plan to take the House of Knights, or Dexterity otherwise. Choose the chill touch, force dart, force weapon and phantom grapnel cantrips, and the Adventurer Arcane Initiation. Take the Blaster or Resistive fighting style, and pick any background that emphasises magical discipline. At 2nd level, take the Blasting Flourish and Mystical Armor.

Hit Points​

Hit Dice: 1d8 per warmage level.

Hit Points at 1st Level: 8 + your Constitution modifier.

Hit Points at Higher Levels: 1d8 (or 5) + your Constitution modifier per warmage level after 1st.

Proficiencies​

Armor: Light armor

Weapons: Simple weapons

Tools: One artisan’s kit of your choice, one gaming set of your choice

Saving Throws: Constitution, Intelligence

Skills: Choose two from Acrobatics, Animal Handling, Arcana, Athletics, History, Investigation, Medicine, Perception and Survival

Equipment​

You start with the following equipment, in addition to the equipment granted by your background:

  • Leather armor, a dagger, and any simple weapon

  • (a) a component pouch or (b) an arcane focus

  • (a) an explorer’s pack or (b) a scholar’s pack

  • A gaming set

Spellcasting​

At 1st level you begin to learn the simple yet potent brand of spellcasting for which warmages are known.

Cantrips​

You know four cantrips of your choice from the warmage spell list. You learn additional warmage cantrips of your choice at higher levels, as shown in the Cantrips Known column of the Warmage table. Additionally, when you gain a level in this class you can replace one warmage cantrip you know with another from the warmage spell list.

Spellcasting Ability​

Intelligence is your spellcasting ability for your warmage spells, since you learn your spells through practice and mental discipline. You use your Intelligence whenever a spell refers to your spellcasting ability, and you use your Intelligence modifier when setting the saving throw DC for a warmage spell and when making an attack roll with one.

Spell save DC = 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Intelligence modifier

Spell attack modifier = your proficiency bonus + your Intelligence modifier

Spellcasting Focus​

You can use an arcane focus as a spellcasting focus for your warmage spells.

Arcane Initiation​

Warmages come from all backgrounds and walks of life. At 1st level, choose where you first learned the basics of magic. The two cantrips your initiation grants do not count against your number of cantrips known.

  • Adventurer. You picked up your magic informally by travelling with a dozen different mages over the years. You learn mage hand and ray of frost.

  • Circus Performer. You learned a few simple tricks to participate in a sideshow act. You learn dancing lights and minor illusion.

  • Eldritch Event. An influx of insidious magic left an imprint on you. You learn chill touch and message.

  • Mercenary. You mastered the fundamentals of war magic to engage in battle with similarly armed arcanists. You learn arc blade and true strike.

  • Self-Taught. You taught yourself the fundamentals from a dusty tome or an abandoned scroll. You learn fire bolt and light.

  • Survival. To stay alive in the wilderness, you taught yourself to cast simple spells. You learn druidcraft and shillelagh.

  • Temple. A monastery or temple educated you in the ways of gentle healing magic. You learn sacred flame and spare the dying.

  • Tower Apprentice. You apprenticed under a spellcaster who taught you the fundamentals of arcana. You learn prestidigitation and shocking grasp.

Arcane Fighting Style​

Warmages learn that magic is the purest of weapons and can be wielded as easily as any other. At 1st level, choose one of the following fighting styles.

  • Blaster. You gain a +1 bonus to the saving throw DCs of your warmage spells.

  • Deflector. When you have at least one hand free and a creature hits you with a spell attack or a ranged weapon attack, you can use your reaction to add 3 to your Armor Class against that attack, potentially causing it to miss.

  • Resistive. While you are wearing light armor or are under the effects of the mage armor spell, you gain a +1 bonus to your Armor Class.

  • Sniper. You gain a +1 bonus to ranged spell attack rolls, and your warmage cantrips ignore half cover.

  • Striker. When you hit a creature with a cantrip that requires a melee attack and you exceed the target’s Armor Class by 5 or more, or you score a critical hit, you can add your proficiency bonus to the damage roll.

Warmage Edge​

Starting at 2nd level, you can channel raw arcane pressure into the cantrips you have perfected. Once on each of your turns when you deal damage with a warmage cantrip, choose one of that cantrip’s damage rolls and add your Intelligence modifier to it.

At 11th level, you can add your Intelligence modifier to two of that cantrip’s damage rolls, and at 17th level, three. If the cantrip has fewer damage rolls than that, add your Intelligence modifier to the same roll for each remaining application.

For example, a 17th-level warmage casting fire bolt makes one damage roll and so deals 4d10 + three times her Intelligence modifier. The same warmage casting magic daggers makes four separate damage rolls, and adds her Intelligence modifier to three of them.

Cantrip Flourishes​

Also at 2nd level, you learn to hang a small piece of battlefield craft off the end of a cantrip. Certain warmage tricks are Flourishes, identified as such in their descriptions. Once on each of your turns, when you deal damage to a creature with a warmage cantrip, you can apply one Flourish you know to that creature.

You can apply only one Flourish on each of your turns, no matter how many you know. A Flourish requires no action and can be applied in the same turn you use Warmage Edge or an Overcharge.

Warmage Tricks​

Beginning at 2nd level, you learn to use warmage tricks: special techniques that alter the way you fight, move and cast your spells. You learn two tricks of your choice for which you meet the prerequisites, and you learn additional tricks as shown in the Tricks Known column of the Warmage table. Flourishes are tricks and count against this number.

When you gain a level in this class, you can choose one of the tricks you know and replace it with another trick for which you meet the prerequisites. A level prerequisite refers to your level in this class.

Warmage House​

Upon reaching 3rd level, you choose a Warmage House that teaches you a suite of magical strategies: the House of Bishops, Fortune, Kings, Knights, Pawns or Rooks. Your choice grants you features at 3rd level and again at 7th, 10th, 15th and 18th level.

Ability Score Improvement​

When you reach 4th level, and again at 8th, 12th, 16th and 19th level, you can increase one ability score of your choice by 2, or you can increase two ability scores of your choice by 1. As normal, you can’t increase an ability score above 20 using this feature.

Overcharge​

Starting at 5th level, you can force more out of a cantrip than it was ever built to carry — not more power, but a different shape entirely. You have a number of Overcharge uses shown in the Overcharge Uses column of the Warmage table. You regain all expended uses when you finish a short or long rest.

When you cast a warmage cantrip, you can expend one use of Overcharge to apply one Overcharge option you know to that casting. You know all of the Lesser Overcharges from 5th level, you learn the Greater Overcharges at 11th level, and you learn the Master Overcharges at 17th level. Where an Overcharge calls for a saving throw, the DC equals your spell save DC.

You can apply only one Overcharge to a single casting of a cantrip until 20th level, and applying an Overcharge does not change the cantrip’s casting time unless the Overcharge says so.

The Overcharge options are described in their own section, following the Flourishes.

Deflective Instinct​

At 6th level, you learn to shed hostile magic rather than merely endure it. When you succeed on a saving throw against a spell or other magical effect that would still deal half damage to you on a successful save, you take no damage from it instead.

Strategic Deflection​

Starting at 14th level, when a creature casts a spell that forces you to make a saving throw to avoid damage, you can use your reaction to attempt to redirect some of the spell’s energy to a new target. If you succeed on your saving throw against the spell, choose another creature you can see within the spell’s range or up to 30 feet away from you, whichever is closer, to make a saving throw against your spell save DC. The chosen creature can be the original spellcaster.

On a failed save, the creature suffers the effects of the spell as if you had cast it and it had been the original target or been within the area of the spell. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

Master Warmage​

At 20th level, you reach the pinnacle of your warmage prowess. You can apply two different Overcharges to the same casting of a cantrip, expending one use of Overcharge for each. In addition, when you roll initiative and have no uses of Overcharge remaining, you regain two uses.

Multiclassing​

To take a level in this class as a multiclass character, you must have an Intelligence score of 13 or higher. You gain proficiency with light armor, simple weapons and one gaming set of your choice. Your warmage cantrips do not count against the number of cantrips you know from other classes, and levels in this class do not contribute to your spellcaster level for the purposes of the Multiclass Spellcaster table — except for levels in the House of Bishops, which count as one third of a level, rounded down, in the same manner as an Eldritch Knight.

Cantrip Flourishes​

Each Flourish below is a warmage trick. You apply one Flourish on each of your turns, when a warmage cantrip you cast deals damage to a creature. Each Flourish requires the cantrip to have dealt damage of the listed type to that creature. Where a Flourish calls for a saving throw, the DC equals your spell save DC.

Blasting Flourish​

Prerequisite: force damage. You push the creature up to 10 feet away from you in a straight line.

Concussive Flourish​

Prerequisite: thunder damage. A 15-foot-diameter sphere of magical silence, as per the silence spell, is centred on the creature or on yourself (your choice) and lasts until the start of your next turn.

Corrosive Flourish​

Prerequisite: acid damage. The acid eats into the creature’s defences. The next attack roll made against it before the start of your next turn treats its Armor Class as 2 lower.

Draining Flourish​

Prerequisite: necrotic or poison damage. You siphon some of the creature’s life force and gain temporary hit points equal to your Intelligence modifier, which last for 1 minute. Temporary hit points from this Flourish don’t stack with themselves.

Explosive Flourish​

Prerequisite: fire damage. The fire detonates. Each creature within 5 feet of the target other than you and the target must succeed on a Dexterity saving throw or take half the fire damage dealt to the target.

Icy Flourish​

Prerequisite: cold damage. Until the start of your next turn, the creature’s speed is reduced by 10 feet and it takes a −2 penalty to the next attack roll it makes.

Radiant Flourish​

Prerequisite: radiant damage. Until the start of your next turn, the creature sheds dim light in a 10-foot radius and can’t benefit from being invisible or from being hidden.

Rending Flourish​

Prerequisite: bludgeoning, piercing or slashing damage. The creature can’t take reactions until the start of your next turn.

Static Flourish​

Prerequisite: lightning damage. You sap part of the energy into a charge that clings to your body until the start of your next turn. While charged, you can use your reaction when you take damage from a creature you can see within 5 feet of you to deal lightning damage equal to your Intelligence modifier to that creature.

Suppressing Flourish​

Prerequisite: 5th level, any damage type. The creature has disadvantage on the next attack roll it makes against a target other than you before the start of your next turn.

Overcharges​

You expend one use of Overcharge to apply one of these options to a warmage cantrip as you cast it. Where an Overcharge calls for a saving throw, the DC equals your spell save DC.

Lesser Overcharges​

You know all of these from 5th level.

Barrage​

If the cantrip requires a single attack roll, make one additional attack roll against a different creature within 15 feet of the original target. On a hit, that creature takes the cantrip’s damage. Warmage Edge can enhance the damage of only one of the two attacks.

Breach​

The cantrip ignores cover, and the target’s resistance to the cantrip’s damage type has no effect against it. If the cantrip damages an object, it deals double damage to it.

Concussion​

The target must succeed on a Strength saving throw or be pushed 10 feet away from you and knocked prone.

Quicken​

You cast the cantrip as a bonus action instead of an action. You can use this Overcharge only once on each of your turns.

Refract​

A cantrip that targets a single creature instead fills a 15-foot cone originating from you. Each creature in the cone must make a Dexterity saving throw, taking the cantrip’s damage on a failed save and half as much damage on a successful one. Warmage Edge can enhance the damage against only one creature. If the cantrip normally requires an attack roll, you make no attack roll when you use this Overcharge.

Snare​

The target must make a Strength or Dexterity saving throw (its choice). On a failed save, its speed becomes 0 until the start of your next turn.

Greater Overcharges​

You learn these at 11th level, in addition to the Lesser Overcharges.

Displace​

After the cantrip resolves, either you or the target teleports up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space that you can see. An unwilling target can resist with a Charisma saving throw.

Overload​

Treat each of the cantrip’s damage dice against one target of your choice as having rolled the highest number possible. You can’t apply this Overcharge to a cantrip that has scored a critical hit.

Rupture​

Until the start of your next turn, the target has disadvantage on the next saving throw it makes and it can’t regain hit points.

Master Overcharges​

You learn these at 17th level, in addition to the Lesser and Greater Overcharges.

Cataclysm​

A cantrip that targets a single creature instead fills a 20-foot-radius sphere centred on a point you can see within the cantrip’s range. Each creature in the area must make a Dexterity saving throw, taking the cantrip’s damage on a failed save and half as much damage on a successful one. Warmage Edge can enhance the damage against only one creature. If the cantrip normally requires an attack roll, you make no attack roll when you use this Overcharge.

Unmake​

Until the end of your next turn, the target’s Armor Class is reduced by 2 and it has disadvantage on attack rolls.

Warmage Tricks​

The tricks below are presented in alphabetical order. The ten Flourishes described earlier are also warmage tricks. If a trick has prerequisites, you must meet them to choose it; you can choose the trick at the same time you meet its prerequisites.

Bishop’s Maneuver​

Prerequisites: 10th level, House of Bishops

You can take the Disengage action as a bonus action. When you do so, your walking speed increases by 10 feet until the end of your turn.

Blinding Light​

Prerequisite: light cantrip

When you use the light cantrip to target an object you are holding, you can direct a flare at a creature you can see within 10 feet of you. That creature must make a Constitution saving throw against your spell save DC, and on a failed save it is blinded until the start of your next turn.

After a creature has failed a saving throw against this trick, it has advantage on all saving throws against it for the next 24 hours.

Castle​

Prerequisites: 10th level, House of Rooks

As an action, you can choose a willing Small or Medium creature you can see within 120 feet of you. You both teleport, swapping places. Once you use this trick, you can’t use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

Chivalrous Presence​

Prerequisite: House of Knights

You gain proficiency in the Insight and Persuasion skills, and you have advantage on ability checks you make to interact with nobility.

Commander’s Steed​

Prerequisite: House of Kings

You learn the find steed spell. You can cast it once without expending a spell slot, and you regain the ability to do so when you finish a long rest. Your steed is more resilient than most: its hit point maximum increases by an amount equal to your warmage level.

Encryptogram​

Prerequisite: cryptogram cantrip

Your knowledge of ciphers has improved your magically clandestine communications. When you cast cryptogram, its limit is 20 characters instead of 8, and only the specified recipient can read the message.

Extended Range​

Whenever you cast a warmage cantrip that has a range of 5 feet or greater, its range is doubled.

Field Medic​

Prerequisite: House of Bishops

You learn the spare the dying cantrip, which doesn’t count against your number of cantrips known. Additionally, when you cast spare the dying on a creature that has 0 hit points, the target regains 1 hit point and gains temporary hit points equal to your warmage level, which last for 1 minute. Once a creature regains hit points from this trick, it can’t do so again until it finishes a long rest.

Flexible Range​

Being within 5 feet of a hostile creature doesn’t impose disadvantage on your ranged spell attack rolls. Additionally, when you cast a warmage cantrip that requires a melee spell attack, its range increases to 10 feet.

Fold​

Prerequisites: 10th level, House of Fortune

You have learned to accept defeat graciously. As a reaction when you are hit by an attack, you can expend three Fate Dice to cast the shield spell without expending a spell slot. Once you use this trick, you can’t use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

Gamble​

Prerequisites: House of Fortune, cheat cantrip

You are always under the effects of the cheat cantrip. Additionally, when you miss with an attack roll or you fail an ability check or saving throw, you can reroll the d20 and must use the new roll. Once you use this ability, you can’t use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

Infinite Variation​

Prerequisite: prestidigitation cantrip

You have become exceptionally skilled at using prestidigitation to mimic other spells. When you cast prestidigitation, you can use it to emulate the effects of any cantrip that doesn’t deal damage from the bard, sorcerer, warlock or wizard spell list, even one you don’t know. To do so, you must succeed on a DC 15 Intelligence (Arcana) check, otherwise the spell fails. Intelligence is your spellcasting ability for a cantrip cast using this trick, and it counts as a warmage cantrip for you.

Knight’s Aegis​

Prerequisites: 10th level, House of Knights, force buckler cantrip

When you cast the force buckler cantrip, it lasts until the end of your next turn and it doesn’t end early if you are hit by an attack.

Leading Edge Tactics​

Prerequisite: House of Kings

You always have a plan when engaging the enemy. Attacks against you have disadvantage during the first round of combat. You can’t use this feature when you are surprised.

Lieutenant’s Demand​

Prerequisites: 10th level, House of Kings

You can cast the command spell without expending a spell slot, using your Intelligence as your spellcasting ability for it. Once you use this trick, you can’t use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

Mage Hand Knack​

Prerequisite: mage hand cantrip

Your skill with mage hand allows you to use it as an extension of yourself. When you cast the spell, and as a bonus action on your subsequent turns, you can use one of the following effects with the hand.

  • Press. The hand pushes against a Large or smaller creature within 5 feet of it. Choose a direction away from that creature. Every foot of movement in that direction while the hand is pressing against it costs the creature 1 extra foot of movement. The hand continues to press until the spell ends or you use your bonus action to use a different effect.

  • Punch. The hand strikes one creature or object within 5 feet of it. Make a melee spell attack for the hand. On a hit, the target takes 1d6 force damage.

  • Seize. The hand grabs a Small or smaller creature and attempts to grapple it. The creature must succeed on a Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check of its choice against your spell save DC or be grappled by the hand. The hand continues to grapple until the target uses an action to break the grapple on its turn, the spell ends, or you use your bonus action to use a different effect.

Minor Shadow Illusion​

Prerequisite: minor illusion cantrip

When you create the image of an object in an unoccupied space using minor illusion, you can fill it with fibres of shadowstuff, causing it to become partially real. No matter what form the semi-real object takes, it must be no larger than a 5-foot cube. It has AC 10 and 5 hit points and it weighs 5 pounds. You can have only one semi-real illusion at a time, and while it exists the cantrip requires your concentration.

The illusion can’t replicate a creature, but it can deal damage to a creature within its 5-foot cube. If the illusion is of an object that could deal damage, a creature that enters the object’s cube for the first time on a turn or begins its turn there must make an Intelligence saving throw against your spell save DC, taking 1d6 damage of a type appropriate to the illusion on a failed save. This damage increases to 2d6 at 5th level, 3d6 at 11th level and 4d6 at 17th level. This damage can’t trigger Warmage Edge, a Flourish or an Overcharge.

Mystical Armor​

You can cast the mage armor spell on yourself at will, without expending a spell slot and without spell components.

Mystical Athlete​

Prerequisite: quickstep or springheel cantrip

When you cast quickstep, your speed increases by 20 feet instead of 10 feet. When you cast springheel, your jumping distance increases by 20 feet instead of 10 feet. If you know both of these cantrips, you can cast both of them as part of the same bonus action.

Mystical Vision​

You can cast the detect magic spell at will, without expending a spell slot.

Mystical Weaponmaster​

Prerequisite: force weapon or magic daggers cantrip

When you roll a 1 on the d20 for an attack roll with force weapon or magic daggers, you can reroll the die and must use the new roll.

Phantom Hookshot​

Prerequisite: phantom grapnel cantrip

You can cast the phantom grapnel cantrip as a bonus action. If you do so, its range is reduced to 15 feet. When you cast the spell as an action, creatures are pulled an extra 10 feet toward you by the spell.

Rapid Fortification​

Prerequisite: mending cantrip

You can cast the mending cantrip as an action or a bonus action. If you cast it as an action, you can use one of the following effects.

  • You restore a single nonmagical object, such as a door, cart or window, to pristine condition if at least half of its parts are present. The object can be no larger than 10 cubic feet, or 1 cubic foot if it is exceptionally complex, such as a clock.

  • You create simple fortifications, such as sealing a door shut, adding wooden planks to a window, or building a short stone wall no larger than 10 cubic feet. You must have the materials present to use this ability.

Severe Cantrip​

When a creature rolls a 1 on a saving throw against one of your warmage cantrips, it automatically fails the save and takes twice the number of damage dice dealt by the spell, as if you had scored a critical hit. The additional damage applies only to the creature that rolled a 1.

Signature Focus​

Prerequisite: 5th level

When you finish a long rest, you can place a unique sigil on a simple or martial weapon, which becomes your signature focus until you use this ability again. The weapon becomes magical, and you can use it as a spellcasting focus for your warmage spells. Your signature focus is bonded to you and gains the following properties.

  • As a bonus action, you can call your signature focus to your hand, as long as you are on the same plane of existence as it.

  • You can add your Intelligence modifier, instead of your Strength or Dexterity modifier, to attack and damage rolls made with it.

  • Your signature focus has a number of charges equal to your Intelligence modifier, minimum 1. Once on each of your turns, when you deal damage to a creature with the focus or with a cantrip cast through it, you can expend 1 charge to deal an extra 1d8 force damage to that creature. The focus regains all expended charges when you finish a long rest.

Snake Eyes​

Prerequisites: 10th level, House of Fortune

If you roll a 1 or a 2 on a Fate Die, you keep the die instead of giving it to the GM.

Unerring Strike​

Prerequisites: 10th level, true strike cantrip

When you cast the true strike cantrip, you can concentrate on it for up to 1 minute. You gain advantage on the first attack roll you make against the target on each of your turns while you maintain concentration on it.

Vanguard Rush​

Prerequisite: House of Knights

Once on each of your turns, when you make a melee attack against a creature you can see, you can move up to 10 feet toward that creature immediately before making the attack. This movement doesn’t provoke opportunity attacks and doesn’t count against your movement for the turn.

Warmage Spells​

Unlike other spellcasters, warmages learn only cantrips, which they pick from the following list as they gain levels in this class. Cantrips marked with an asterisk are described in full in this document; the rest are standard cantrips found in the core rules. If a cantrip appears on the wizard spell list that is not represented here, the GM can allow it to be a warmage spell as well.

The cantrips granted by an Arcane Initiation — dancing lights, druidcraft, message, sacred flame, shillelagh and spare the dying — are also warmage spells for you if your initiation grants them.

Warmage Houses​

Each warmage is defined by their chosen House, which teaches them valuable skills and offers a confederation of allies they will keep for the rest of their lives. Five are named for the pieces of the board; the sixth is named for the wheel.

  • House of Bishops
  • House of Fortune
  • House of Kings
  • House of Knights
  • House of Pawns
  • House of Rooks
