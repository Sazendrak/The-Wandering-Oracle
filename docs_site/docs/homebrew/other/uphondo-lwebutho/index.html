Uphondo Lwebutho

Uphondo Lwebutho​

"Horn of the Regiment." Uphondo is the horn — the same word used for the horn of an ox, and for the two wings of warriors that run wide and close behind an enemy while the chest holds them in place. Ibutho is the regiment: an age-set, everyone who came of age in the same season, trained together and sent into the field together. Together the name means something closer to one of the horns than a warrior who has a horn — you are not a soldier who belongs to a regiment, you are a piece of it, and the piece that moves. Plural, the tradition is called the izimpondo zebutho, the regiment's horns.

They do not wear armor. They wear a shield taller than a man and a spear shorter than an arm, and they arrive a day before anyone expects them. A regiment fights as one body: the chest holds the enemy in place, the horns run wide and close behind them, and the loins sit with their backs turned, waiting to be spent. A single warrior of this tradition carries the whole doctrine in their bones — hook the shield, open the ribs, stab once, call out.

In the Realms​

Faerûn's closest parallel is the Shaar — the enormous savanna sweeping east from the Lake of Steam to the Eastern Shaar, where grassland nations rise and fall on the strength of their regiments rather than their walls. Shaaran warriors live and die by mobility across open ground, and a discipline built on encirclement, forced marches, and the shield line belongs there more naturally than anywhere in the North.

South and west, the tradition shades into other things. On the Chultan Peninsula, the same drill contracts to jungle warfare — shorter sightlines, ambush instead of encirclement, the horns closing through undergrowth. Further south still, across the Great Sea, lies Katashaka, the continent almost nothing in the North has mapped; regiments trained in this style occasionally arrive from there with no interest in explaining themselves.

A warrior of this tradition on the Sword Coast is almost always far from home; a veteran of a Shaaran ibutho who marched north with a caravan, or a Chultan raised to the shield in Port Nyanzaru and hired out of it.

3rd Level - Ukuzivocavoca Kwebutho (Regimental Drill)​

Unarmored Defense. While you aren't wearing armor, your AC equals the higher of 12 + your Strength modifier, or 10 + your Dexterity modifier + your Strength modifier. You can use a Shield.

You lose this benefit while a magic item is setting or increasing your Strength or Dexterity; a permanent increase you have already consumed, such as a Manual of Gainful Exercise, is your own.

Spear and Shield. While wielding a Shield and a Spear or Javelin, that weapon deals 1d8 in one hand and its thrown ranges are doubled.

3rd Level - Izimpondo ZeNyathi (Horns of the Buffalo)​

Once per turn when you hit with a melee weapon, you can mark the target Encircled until the end of your next turn. Only one creature at a time; marking a new one ends the old.

An Encircled creature has its Speed reduced by 10 feet and can't Disengage. Once on each of your turns, when you or an ally hits it while you are both within 5 feet of it, that attack deals extra damage equal to your Proficiency Bonus.

7th Level - Bamba Isihlangu (Hook the Shield)​

Once on each of your turns when you hit an Encircled creature, you can hook its guard aside (no action required). It makes a Strength save (DC 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your Strength modifier). On a failure, until the start of your next turn it gains no benefit from a Shield or Cover, and the next attack against it has Advantage.

7th Level - Uhambo Olude (Barefoot March)​

You and up to five companions can travel at a Fast pace without the penalty to passive Perception, and can force march extra hours equal to your Constitution modifier (minimum 1) without saving against Exhaustion. You have Advantage on saves against Exhaustion from heat, thirst, or marching.

10th Level - Bamba bese uhlasela (Chest and Loins)​

Hold the Chest. Enemies leaving your reach treat that space as Difficult Terrain.

Send the Loins. As a Bonus Action, choose up to two allies within 30 feet who can see or hear you. Each can use their Reaction to move up to half their Speed without provoking Opportunity Attacks, ending closer to your Encircled creature if able. Uses equal to your Proficiency Bonus, regained on a Short or Long Rest.

15th Level - USUTHU! (Frightful Warcry)​

As a Bonus Action, each enemy within 30 feet that can see or hear you makes a Wisdom save (DC 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your Strength modifier) or is Frightened of you for 1 minute, the creature can make the save again at the end of each of their turns. Your Encircled creature saves with Disadvantage. Until the start of your next turn, the first attack each ally makes against your Encircled creature has Advantage. Once per Short or Long Rest.

18th Level - NGADLA! (Cry of Victory)​

Your Encircled damage rider no longer needs an ally — it applies whenever you are within 5 feet of the target — and it triggers on every hit rather than once per turn.

When you reduce a creature to 0 Hit Points with a melee attack, you can make one additional attack against a different creature: a melee attack within reach, or a thrown Spear or Javelin within normal range, drawing the weapon as part of the attack. You can move half your Speed first without provoking Opportunity Attacks, and the attack applies Encircled on a hit. Uses equal to your Proficiency Bonus, regained on a Long Rest.
